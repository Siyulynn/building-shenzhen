## M3b → M4 design implications

- **M3b: separate site eligibility from delivery.** Keep zoning, offers, reservations and factory production. A factory should open when its premises actually finish, not when the original estimate expires. Siteability in the investment-only test can use a permissive servicing fixture. Retain a limited existing-premises route for small processing contracts.
- **M4: one monthly formal construction pool.** Earthworks, streets, pipes, standard factories and landmarks compete for the same capacity. Express demand as normalized crew-work rather than treating a kilometre of road as comparable to a square metre of floor. A project can still require a specialist or equipment without creating another pool. Use a small set of priority levels and show whether each job is waiting for capacity, finance or site access.
- **Two contract modes.** A mobilized general crew adds time-limited capacity to the pool. A dedicated contract supplies the assigned project's work outside the pool; it uses no shared capacity but still has a mobilization time, a finite work rate and a payer. Funding and delivery are separate: an investor paying for a water line does not automatically bring a crew. Do not give the same crew both a pool bonus and a project exemption. Lufeng on first-stage Shennan is the natural dedicated example. The Engineering Corps can add assignable capacity in waves; its transfer to construction enterprises changes control and funding, not headcount a second time.
- **三通一平 is the playable minimum, not a universal historical label.** Track road connection, usable water and power, and cleared/levelled ground for the portion of the zone being developed. Completed trunk links can serve several sites without buying the same road twice. Work can be publicly commissioned or delivered under a specific contract. Land clearance still needs rights and compensation; leveling does not settle those. Distinguish physical connection from spare supply: a connected factory can still face a power shortage. Keep drainage, communications and other historical stages in project descriptions or later upgrades rather than adding citywide networks to the slice.
- **Village self-build does not draw from the formal pool.** Keep separate household limits for plot space, local effort and demand. Let demand for cheap rooms, meals and everyday goods trigger rooms, small shops and market conversions without making the city queue them. A shop conversion moves existing floor between uses; it does not create extra floor or beds. Early villages should visibly relieve shortages before sanitation and crowding become binding; permitted village building and informality are not the same thing. Keep works-camp beds separate so neither a dedicated contractor nor a village build takes formal capacity by accident.

## First M4 test

Run the same early programme three ways: **local pool only**, **Lufeng-style dedicated road contract**, and **general crew added to the pool**. With a road and factory queued together, the dedicated deal should free the shared pool for the factory without making the road instant or free; mobilizing extra general capacity should help either job. In all three runs, village rooms and shops should still emerge when there is demand. Track finished serviced land, work delivered, cash paid, camps and beds provided separately. Before setting exact capacity, look for actual starts and completions, annual labour and equipment counts by contractor, and the original Shangbu loan/presale terms.
