# Shenzhenxu's east gate and the name “东门” / 深圳墟东门与“东门”地名

**Status:** reading note, 2026-11-23. The source is an original-research essay by 宝安县土著中, published on 新安历史文化研究 (WeChat) on 2025-01-01; the author says it appeared in 《深圳史志》. The print issue has not been inspected. The argument is a **lead for M3**, not a surveyed reconstruction.

## 1. The question

The old market is now called **东门** (East Gate), not 南门, 北门 or 西门. The article asks where the east gate actually was, and why its name came to stand for the whole of 深圳墟.

The conventional answer — “the east gate was busy, so the name spread” — does not fit the map. The gate marked 东门 on the 1948/1949 maps sits at **解放路 × 东门中路**, an edge that was still thinly built in the 1990s. The article argues that this was the **new** east gate, and that the **old** east gate stood at **十字街**, the crossing of 人民北路 and 解放路.

## 2. The argument

1. **The four gates are offset.** On the 1948/1949 maps, 南门 is at the south end of 南庆街 and 北门 at the west side of mid-新园路. If the marked 东门 at 解放路 × 东门中路 were the original centre-line gate, the north and south gates would sit too far west. Put the old east gate at 十字街 and the four gates line up.
2. **The 炮楼 is on 人民北路.** A defensive tower belongs on a boundary, not in the middle of a market. The article reads 人民北路 as the early eastern edge and the tower as its guard.
3. **The “东新” street names.** East of 十字街 are 东新后街, 东新横街 and 东新街 — “east-new” lanes outside the old gate. They mark the market's eastward expansion. If 十字街 had always been the centre, those names would make no sense.
4. **叶屋村 was outside.** The Jiaqing gazetteer records 叶屋村, and the Zhang lineage of 湖贝 owned the market. The article argues the village lay outside the early market and was only enclosed by the Republican expansion.
5. **Expansion could only go east.** Water and the railway blocked the west and northwest; 南塘 and 向南 blocked the south. The market grew east, from the old gate at 十字街 to the new gate at 解放路 × 东门中路.
6. **The name stuck.** 十字街 became the busiest part of the enlarged market, but people kept saying “去东门” for it. After 1979, “深圳” meant the whole city; 廖虹雷 recalls that the formal name “东门” spread during the 1984 first-phase redevelopment, to avoid confusion with 深圳市.

The article is careful to say it does **not** decide whether the gates were physical gates, ward entrances or market entrances. It only locates the eastern entrance.

## 3. What it changes for M3

- **The early market core is 十字街, not the 1948 “东门” mark.** For the 1979 scene, 十字街 (人民北路 × 解放路) is the strongest candidate for the oldest commercial centre. The 1948/1949 gate labels describe the Republican layout, not the Qing one.
- **人民北路 is the early eastern edge.** That gives a plausible boundary for the first market and a reason for the 炮楼's position.
- **The eastward expansion zone is legible.** 东新后街 → 东新横街 → 东新街 → the new gate at 解放路 × 东门中路 gives a direction of growth that can be tested against the 1977 street map and the KH-9 mosaic.
- **The 1948/1949 street names are usable as topology.** 维新街 (today's 人民北路), 谷行街 (today's 解放路), 西和街, 三角街, 东新街, 南庆街, 南塘村 and 叶屋村 give a named skeleton. They do not give footprints.
- **The Buji River has moved.** The article says it was straightened decades ago and no longer runs past the old street. The 1979 channel therefore needs its own source; do not trace today's channel back into the opening scene.

## 5. Limits

- **Original research, not a primary source.** The gate argument is an interpretation. The 1953 county-seat move corroborates the existing `site.county_offices.exists_1979` claim; the 1931 town date still needs a gazetteer or a period document.
- **Print version unchecked.** The author says the essay appeared in 《深圳史志》; we have only the WeChat copy. The issue, date and page are unknown.
- **No ground survey.** The modern-location figure is a visual comparison, not a measured georeference.
- **Images are reference-only.** The author explicitly forbids reuse without permission. Do not put these figures in the game, a public viewer or a texture.
- **The Buji River channel is unresolved.** The article says it was straightened, but gives no date or alignment. Use the 1977 street map and the KH-9 mosaic for the 1979 channel.
